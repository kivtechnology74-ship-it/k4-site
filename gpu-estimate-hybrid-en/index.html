Model balance and annual metrics

SOC is the energy remaining in the battery. If final SOC differs from initial SOC, cost is adjusted for the change in stored energy: the value per kWh is fuel cost divided by gas generation, or, when there is no gas generation, the grid tariff. If the adjustment is below the original costs are considered comparable. At or above the threshold, “Not comparable” is shown while the adjusted amount remains available as a reference.

Calculation rules and boundaries

Each scenario defines whether grid, gas generation, solar PV, and BESS are used. Solar PV serves load and charges the battery; the battery discharges down to its reserve; gas generation covers remaining load and may charge the battery when spare capacity is available; the grid serves the remaining load only in grid-connected scenarios. Hourly balance: sources and unserved load equal demand, battery charging, and curtailed solar. Grid export is not modeled: excess PV is curtailed and energy sales are excluded.

For catalog profiles with specific or volumetric fuel rates, consumption is determined from actual fleet load, assuming an even load split across identical units. The model linearly interpolates between OEM points and does not extrapolate beyond them. Specific consumption is multiplied by actual generation once. Volumetric consumption is calculated from the curve; conversion from standard cubic feet per hour is applied once. Manually entered fuel use is for one engine at full load per hour and is scaled to its actual output. Hourly dispatch is not optimized; part-load curves, starts, output changes, minimum run time, and downtime are not modeled.
