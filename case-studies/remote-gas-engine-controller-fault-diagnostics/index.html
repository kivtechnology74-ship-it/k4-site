Multiple Controller Alarms, One Electrical Fault

Remote fault isolation of a gas-engine control system using photographs, field measurements and AI-assisted engineering analysis.

FIELD DIAGNOSTICS24 VDCCONTROL SYSTEMREMOTE SUPPORT
K4 TechnologyAnonymized field diagnostic case

On an operating gas-engine installation, simultaneous controller alarms appeared across the Baudouin LECM, Altronic IGS-PTM and ComAp control system. Part of the 24 VDC control system was also operating abnormally.

The Site Constraint

A specialist could not be mobilised to the site immediately. A site technician was available with a smartphone and multimeter, could send photographs and could perform simple measurements.

Diagnostic Method

K4 reviewed photographs of control cabinets, labels, fuses and terminals, then compared alarms from the different subsystems. A sequence of measurements was developed and the diagnostic hypothesis was reviewed after each result. The work separated independent 24 VDC circuits and excluded directions that the available evidence did not support.

01 / REVIEW

Photographs, alarm messages, labels, fuses and terminals were reviewed together.

02 / COMPARE

Alarms across the engine, ignition and ComAp control subsystems were compared.

03 / MEASURE

Measurements were requested in sequence; each result informed the next diagnostic step.

04 / REFINE

Independent 24 VDC circuits were distinguished and unsupported fault paths were excluded.

Finding 1 — Common Supply Problem

The evidence identified a defective isolation switch. After it was replaced, approximately 25 VDC was restored across most affected circuits.

Finding 2 — Residual C105–C106 Fault

After the common supply issue was addressed, the remaining issue was narrowed to the C105–C106 circuit group. The associated fuses were intact, but supply voltage was not reaching the fuse inputs.

The residual fault was narrowed for further investigation. The available information did not establish its complete cause or confirm that it was fully resolved.

What Was Excluded

The available evidence did not support treating the simultaneous alarms as multiple independent controller failures.

The alarms were treated as evidence to compare across subsystems, not as proof that each named controller had independently failed.

Why This Case Matters

Multiple alarms can appear together when the fault area involves a shared or related supply path. In this case, comparing subsystem alarms with cabinet photographs and sequential measurements helped distinguish the defective isolation switch from the remaining C105–C106 circuit issue.

The Remote Diagnostic Model

Technician + Smartphone + Multimeter + Photos & measurements + Remote engineering analysisThe site technician provided local observations and measurements while K4 reviewed the evidence remotely.

Commercial Consequence

The diagnostic process reduced the fault scope before expensive electronic modules were treated as failed components.

Decision Boundary

This case documents remote fault isolation, not a claim that every residual fault was completely resolved remotely.

Actions at site remain subject to the plant's procedures and the judgement of appropriately qualified and authorised personnel.
