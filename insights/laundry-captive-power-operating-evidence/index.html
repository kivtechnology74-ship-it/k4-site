Continuous Laundry Duty: Compare Operating Evidence Before Modelling Returns

A similar recalled engine package had a different reported operating trajectory; measured performance and owner economics remain open questions.

The interview describes high running hours and few out-of-cycle supplier contacts, but neither plant availability nor realised savings has been verified.

Context and decision question

Reported: A former supplier-side participant described a municipal laundry using two 100 kWe gas-converted engines in a container, with recalled owner investment of RUB 9 million. The business operated around the clock with long washing cycles, typical loading of 75–85%, and approximately 7,500–8,000 hours per engine per year. The participant described careful day-to-day operation and recalled one out-of-cycle supplier contact over about two years, concerning a controller password. This is a report about supplier contacts, not a measured uptime, fault count or availability rate. The previous supplier supplied and serviced the plant; K4's work is a 2026 interview-based retrospective, without historical execution or audit.

Decision question: What do recalled loading and hours allow us to estimate, and what additional evidence is needed before the owner can rely on a generation or cash-saving figure?

Reconstruction and heat choice

Calculated: The 200 kWe installed rating and reported loading imply a 150–170 kW combined operating band. Multiplying the low and high bands by recalled engine hours gives 1.125–1.360 GWh/year; the midpoint convention is 1.240 GWh/year. These are arithmetic boundaries, not metered export. A round-the-clock process does not mean continuous engine operation; no engine availability can be derived from the supplier call count.

Reported: Heat was bought from a municipal network at a preferential tariff, and the gas-engine plant did not include heat recovery. Without heat demand, tariff and incremental CHP cost, this does not prove that heat recovery would have been uneconomic.

Illustrative economic sensitivity

Illustrative scenario: The workbook assumes a RUB 5.00/kWh fully avoidable grid tariff and RUB 2.20/kWh all-in generation cost at the 1.240 GWh/year calculated midpoint. It then gives RUB 3.472 million/year annual benefit and 2.59 years simple payback. If only 80% of that grid tariff is avoidable, with all else unchanged, simple payback becomes 4.03 years. Neither tariff, all-in cost nor tax basis is documented for this site. The result is not a verified forecast, achieved savings or historical return.

Decision and limitations

Request interval demand, engine and revenue meters, fuel and heat bills, power-quality tests, O&M and event logs, and evidence of actual service cost and avoidable grid charges. Use the existing Gas Engine Advisory for Power Developers for a current owner-side decision. CTA: Discuss the operating evidence for your site.

No project year or location, measured fuel performance, plant availability, tariff, financial accounts or actual return was established. This interview is not independently verified; the historical asset was not a K4 project.
