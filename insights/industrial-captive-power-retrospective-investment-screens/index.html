What this series examines

An attractive difference between grid and on-site electricity costs is only the start of a captive-power screen. An owner also needs to know the process duty, realistic engine loading, hours that can actually be run, grid support, fuel and service scope, major-overhaul exposure, and the capital and taxes included in each cost figure.

These accounts concern historical installations supplied and serviced by a previous organisation. K4's work is a 2026 retrospective interpretation of participant interviews. K4 did not deliver or audit the assets and did not review primary operating logs, invoices or independently corroborate the interviews. The articles separate Reported, Calculated and Illustrative scenario material. Their purpose is to identify the next evidence required for a prospective owner decision, not to establish historical returns.

Six investment questions

  1. Industrial plastics: What does a remembered payback period establish when original cash flows are unavailable? Read the plastics manufacturer screen.
  2. Electroplating: Can a generator follow a defined process window, and which grid charges would actually be avoided? Read the electroplating screen.
  3. Variable manufacturing load: How should an investor test fluctuating demand, network quality and overhaul exposure? Read the variable-load screen.
  4. Continuous laundry duty: What can a reported load and run-hour range establish about output and returns? Read the laundry screen.
  5. Plastics pipes CHP: What evidence is needed before recovered heat is credited to an investment case? Read the CHP screen.
  6. Mining power: When can a lower grid offer and debt cost change the answer to a 25 MW proposal? Read the mining-power screen.

A paired comparison, not a causal experiment

Reported: The manufacturing and laundry accounts describe a similar two-unit engine configuration and recalled capital outlay, but different process schedules, loading and supplier support experience. Calculated: Their published energy and cost illustrations use different evidence and assumptions. The accounts cannot establish that the load shape, grid, shift pattern or housekeeping caused a particular failure or financial result. A comparison would require interval load data, power-quality records, event and maintenance logs, repair analysis, invoices and matching VAT bases.

From screen to owner decision

For a live project, start with the measured load curve and site constraints; obtain the tariff breakdown, fuel contract, equipment and service scope, and realistic maintenance plan; then test a transparent lifecycle model. Gas Engine Advisory for Power Developers describes K4's existing owner-side scope. CTA: Discuss your power project — send the load profile, grid bills, fuel details and equipment options for a scoped review.

Limitations: All historical operating descriptions remain interview reports. Financial illustrations are not independent audits, investment advice, current quotations or confirmed forecasts. Comparing an electricity tariff including VAT with generation cost excluding VAT, or vice versa, can create a false spread.
