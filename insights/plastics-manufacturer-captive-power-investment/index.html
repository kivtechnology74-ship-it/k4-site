Industrial Plastics Captive Power: Reconstructing the Investment Case

A plastics manufacturer used two gas-engine retrofits alongside the grid and recovered heat seasonally. The reported investment recovery is a management recollection, not a verified cash-flow result.

An anonymised historical account of industrial captive power. The former employer and site are not identified. K4 prepared this retrospective analysis in 2026; K4 did not deliver or audit the project.

The investment question

Could on-site gas generation reduce purchased-electricity exposure at a continuous plastics operation while retaining the grid for standby supply, and could recovered heat add value without overstating the return?

What the participant reported

Reported: The manufacturer operated continuously and used two diesel-origin generator sets converted to gas. The grid remained available as standby supply. Seasonal heat recovery served office and production areas. Management recalled electricity as a material share of manufacturing cost and described reconciling separate operating and finance analyses before the owner approved the investment.

Reported recollection: The former Operations Director recalled investment recovery in approximately two years against a maximum approval threshold of three and a half years. This is not supported here by original cash-flow statements, bills or accounting records.

What the retrospective screen shows

Calculated: K4 reconstructed a gross electricity-cost comparison from approximate historical tariffs, assumed operating hours and modelled output. It is a transparent screening calculation, not measured historical savings. Its gross simple-payback arithmetic does not reconcile to the remembered cash recovery; the difference is unresolved and should not be treated as a loss, saving or proven project return.

The quoted alternatives also differed materially in offered capacity. Their capital-cost difference cannot be read as a like-for-like saving without normalising output, scope and operating duty.

Investment implication: A favourable grid-versus-generation spread is only a first screen. The owner needs to reconcile actual generated and displaced kWh, avoidable grid charges, fuel cost, overhaul and routine maintenance, seasonal heat use, taxes and VAT treatment.

What an investor should verify

  • Original utility bills and the charge components that generation could actually avoid.
  • Generator output, gas-meter readings, operating logs and auxiliary consumption.
  • CAPEX invoices and the scope and capacity of each compared option.
  • Maintenance and overhaul invoices, including whether historic cost averages already include major work.
  • Thermal metering, seasonal demand and the displaced heating cost.
  • Owner cash flows, tax treatment and VAT recoverability before quoting payback, NPV or IRR.

Evidence boundary

This is a retrospective account from a former employee, prepared by K4 in 2026. The employer and site are anonymised. Primary project, utility, operating and accounting records were not reviewed. The reported recovery period is recollection only; reconstructed gross-cost arithmetic is not audited cash payback. This case is not a K4-delivered project, independent audit, asset valuation or investment recommendation.
