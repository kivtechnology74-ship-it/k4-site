K4 Power Decisions · Generator ratings

The Generator Is Rated 2 MW. How Much Power Will My Site Actually Get?

A quoted rating, site output and power delivered at the handover meter are different figures. Here is what to read in the offer before comparing suppliers.

Igor Shibanov, CEO, K4-TECHPublished 25 November 2026

Start with the number in the offer. Then follow it through the rating class, project conditions, gas specification and plant loads to the contractual delivery point. The example values below are marked illustrative; no model-specific output is claimed.

01 · The number on the offer

DIRECTOR“The offer says 2 MW. Is that the power we can use?”

ADVISER“Only if the rating basis and delivery point say so.”

Check the boundary.A generator-terminal rating is not automatically net power available at the buyer’s handover meter.

02 · Read kVA, kW and the rating

DIRECTOR“The offer lists 2,000 kVA. Is that 2 MW?”

ADVISER“At 0.8 power factor, it is 1,600 kW.”

Illustrative offer only
2,000 kVA × 0.8 = 1,600 kW
kVA is apparent power. kW is active electrical power. This is an arithmetic example, not an OEM rating.
Find the duty rating in the offer.ISO 8528-1:2018 includes COP, PRP, LTP, ESP and DCP classifications, plus MAX for low-power sets. For PRP, the 24-hour average limit is 70% unless the engine manufacturer agrees otherwise.

03 · Use the site design point

DIRECTOR“The data sheet uses 25°C. Our Gulf site design point is 45°C. What should I compare?”

ADVISER“Use the offered engine’s correction curve at our design point. Do not guess a derate.”

Reference conditions are stated, not universal site conditions.ISO 15550:2016 gives reference conditions of 25°C, 100 kPa and 30% relative humidity for engine power declarations. Check the exact basis in the offered data sheet and request the supplier’s written correction at the project design point.

04 · Ask what the gas contains

DIRECTOR“The supply sheet says 92% methane. Is that enough?”

ADVISER“No. Ask for methane number, LHV and the required pressure range at the engine inlet.”

Methane content is not methane number.Methane number is calculated from the full gas composition using the specified method. Compare that result, the lower heating value and the inlet-pressure window with the exact engine requirements.

05 · Build the net-output answer

DIRECTOR“What number should the offer give me at the meter?”

ADVISER“Follow the rating through the site correction and plant loads.”

One data sheet gives the first step.The supplier must state the two project-specific inputs below.
  1. Step 1 · From the data sheetJGS 616 GS-N.L (natural gas) · 2,433 kW electrical at p.f. 1.0, as specified.
    ISO 8528 COP/PRP class not stated in the data sheet.
  2. Step 2 · Supplier must stateCorrection at the project design point: _____ kW
  3. Step 3 · Supplier must stateAuxiliaries included / excluded and net auxiliary load: _____ kW
  4. Result · Net at delivery pointNet kW at the contract delivery point: _____ kW

This data sheet is a different machine from the 2 MW offer above. The method is the same at any size: the data sheet gives Step 1, the supplier must give Steps 2 and 3. The specification states 2,433 kW electrical at p.f. 1.0; this is not net site output.

Note the two conventions in this story: the genset offer in Frame 02 is stated in kVA at p.f. 0.8, this engine data sheet states kW at p.f. 1.0. Both are correct. They are not the same quantity — which is exactly why the basis has to be written down before you compare.

06 · Compare the same quantity

ADVISER“Put both offers on the same site, fuel, duty and delivery-point basis.”

DIRECTOR“Then I can compare guaranteed net kW with guaranteed net kW.”

Keep the comparison like for like.Do not compare one supplier’s alternator-terminal rating with another supplier’s net export guarantee.

07 · Name the acceptance test

ADVISER“ISO 8528-6:2023 specifies test methods for the complete generating set.”

DIRECTOR“Then the contract must say what is guaranteed, where it is measured and how the test is run.”

Write three items into the contract.Guaranteed power at the stated site conditions; the measurement point; and the agreed acceptance-test method and criteria.

08 · Ask for a buyer-side review

DIRECTOR“What should I send with the offer?”

ADVISER“The inputs that define the guarantee—and the point where it is measured.”

CHECKABLE FACTSend one line to every supplier:
“State the guaranteed net kW at our delivery point, at our design temperature and gas specification, with the acceptance test to ISO 8528-6 named in the contract.”

If an offer cannot answer that sentence, it is not comparable with one that can.
