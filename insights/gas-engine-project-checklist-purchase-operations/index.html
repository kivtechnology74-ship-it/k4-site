How to use this checklist

Start with the decision that must be made now. For each gate, separate verified evidence from assumptions, identify the consequence of proceeding without missing evidence and record the next action. The useful outcomes are deliberately simple: proceed, proceed with written conditions, obtain specialist verification or stop until the basis changes.

The checklist follows a complete project lifecycle. It is designed for buyers, owners, investors and lenders reviewing gas-engine assets or projects, not as a universal maintenance schedule or a substitute for project-specific engineering.

The six connected evidence gates

01

Define the project decision

State the required output, operating mode, timeline, risk owners and the evidence threshold before equipment commitment.

02

Verify equipment before purchase

Reconcile serial-specific identity, configuration, hours, preservation, visible condition, inspection access and exclusions.

03

Test project and financial assumptions

Connect fuel, site conditions, configuration, auxiliaries, O&M and sensitivity assumptions to one defensible basis.

04

Control delivery and acceptance

Record receipt condition, storage, installation prerequisites, commissioning hold points and final acceptance criteria.

05

Establish the operating baseline

Retain unit-specific runtime, production, meter, alarm, event, downtime and configuration-change records.

06

Maintain the asset deliberately

Use a configuration-specific maintenance framework, risk-based spares and operating evidence to update the plan.

What the PDF helps you record

  • The decision or commercial commitment at each gate.
  • The evidence gap that could change that decision.
  • The owner and due date for the next verification action.
  • Commercial red flags that should not be hidden inside general risk language.
  • The boundary between remote document review and physical inspection.

When remote review is sufficient — and when it is not

Remote review is suitable for bounded document, data and model questions. It can test whether evidence is internally consistent, whether material assumptions are traceable and whether the next commitment is supported.

Physical inspection or specialist field support may be required when reliable records cannot establish installed condition, internal condition, equipment identity, commissioning response, meter boundaries or site constraints.
