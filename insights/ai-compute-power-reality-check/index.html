AI infrastructure is often discussed in GPUs, models, networking and cooling. Once a project moves from cloud consumption to dedicated capacity, another budget becomes decisive: the power budget. Grid access, time-to-power, fuel, dependable generation, maintenance states and recovery behaviour determine whether the compute plan is physically executable.

“Powered land” is not the same as dependable power. Nameplate MW is not the same as compute-ready MW.

Compute MW ≠ installed MW

The useful number sits at the electrical boundary serving the compute load. Installed generator nameplate is only the start; site derating, parasitic loads, planned maintenance, forced outages, common auxiliaries and the chosen redundancy policy all change what can be treated as dependable.

The boundary must be explicit.

Ask whether the quoted output is gross or net, at which ambient conditions it applies, what the plant consumes internally, and which units or shared systems may be unavailable at the same time.

Five questions before compute CAPEX

  1. Is the fuel real?

    Confirm composition, pressure, flow, continuity, treatment scope and the schedule for a usable connection—not only a nominal gas resource.

  2. Is the electrical output net or gross?

    Define the delivery boundary and deduct generation auxiliaries, gas treatment, cooling, transformers and site loads before calling MW compute-ready.

  3. What happens during maintenance?

    Model routine services and major overhauls by unit and by fleet, including common auxiliaries and simultaneous maintenance constraints.

  4. How fast can failed capacity return?

    Test detection, isolation, restart, black-start or grid-recovery logic, critical-spares access and the practical path from a trip back to stable load.

  5. Can the fleet support compute duty continuously?

    Connect load behaviour, unit sizing, redundancy, forced-outage assumptions, commissioning readiness and lifecycle state into one operating model.

Why this matters now

The International Energy Agency says grids are emerging as a critical bottleneck and reports more than 2,500 GW of generation, storage and large-load projects — including data centres — stalled in connection queues worldwide. The U.S. Energy Information Administration describes data-centre load as an emerging dominant driver of long-term U.S. electricity growth. In a higher-demand scenario, EIA also sees substantially more natural-gas generation than in its baseline forecast.

The conclusion is not “gas engines are always the answer.” The conclusion is that power architecture has become a first-order AI infrastructure decision and needs to be tested before compute, land and generation commitments become expensive to reverse.

A useful first screen starts with three inputs

Send the location, target electrical MW and target COD. Add any known grid, gas or site information if available. The first objective is not to sell a machine; it is to identify which assumptions are supported, which remain unknown and which could break the compute plan.
