Jenbacher Borescope Inspection: What Cylinder, Liner and Head Condition Can Tell You

What cylinder, liner and head evidence can show, what it cannot establish, and how it should affect a purchase decision.

Igor Shibanov · Founder and Technical AdvisorPublished: 3 October 2026Last reviewed: 3 October 2026
FIELD EVIDENCEK4 CASE STUDYOWNER-SIDE REVIEW

Key takeaways

  • A borescope inspection shows the visible internal surfaces of a gas engine's combustion space — mainly the cylinder liner, and partly the piston crown and valve area — without dismantling the engine.
  • It cannot show the crankshaft, main or connecting-rod bearings, and it cannot measure remaining service life.
  • A Jenbacher J320 has 20 cylinders. A handful of clean frames says nothing about the other cylinders. Every cylinder must be recorded and labelled.
  • Compressed messenger photos hide defects. Keep original-resolution video.
  • Borescope evidence becomes decision-grade only when combined with oil analysis, compression or leak-down data and a verified service history.

What is a borescope inspection of a gas engine?

A borescope inspection is a visual examination of the inside of an engine's cylinders using a thin optical probe with a camera. K4-Technology introduces the probe through the spark-plug opening. On a used gas engine such as a Jenbacher J320 or J420, this allows the visible liner surface, piston crown and accessible cylinder-head areas to be examined without removing the cylinder head.

What can a borescope show on a used Jenbacher?

AreaWhat borescope can showTypical questions it answers
Cylinder liner surfaceHoning pattern, scoring, scratches, polishing, corrosion, depositsIs the liner surface in a normal wear state? Is there visible damage?
Piston crownDeposits, erosion, visible damageAre there signs of abnormal combustion or overheating?
Valve area (partly)Visible deposits and abnormalities at the valve faces, depending on access and angleIs anything visibly wrong in the valve area?
Cylinder-to-cylinder consistencyWhether all cylinders look alikeIs one cylinder, or one bank, different from the rest?

A visible cross-hatch honing pattern on the liner — the fine criss-cross finish applied during manufacture or reconditioning — is generally a sign that the liner surface has not been heavily worn or polished in the area inspected. Deep vertical scoring, burn marks, spalling or heavy deposits are warning signs that justify further investigation.

What can a borescope not show?

A borescope inspection through the spark-plug opening cannot show:

  • crankshaft condition;
  • main bearings and connecting-rod bearings;
  • camshaft and most of the valve train;
  • turbocharger internals (unless inspected separately);
  • the lower part of the liner hidden by the piston position;
  • total engine lifetime or remaining service life.

A clean borescope result is therefore evidence that the visible combustion-space surfaces are in acceptable condition. It is not a certificate that the engine is healthy.

What did a 20-cylinder J320 borescope inspection teach us?

In April 2025, K4-Technology reviewed a used Jenbacher J320 offered within an anonymized multi-unit gas-engine project. The seller described the unit as practically new but provided no operating-hour record, serial-specific documentation or service history at first contact.

K4-Technology arranged a site visit and engaged a specialist technician to perform a borescope inspection of the cylinder assembly. Each cylinder was marked by number directly on the engine and recorded individually. The visit produced more than 200 photographs and video clips.

What the reviewed material showed:

  • The frames reviewed showed a normal cross-hatch honing pattern on the liners and no obvious deep scoring, burn marks or spalling.
  • One frame showed a vertical light line across the liner surface. From a single blurred still frame it was not possible to tell whether this was a real scratch or a reflection of the borescope light. Only the original video of that cylinder could resolve it.
  • No serial number, confirmed operating hours, oil analysis or compression data for this specific unit were obtained.

How it affected the decision: the inspection gave the buyer side a technical basis to negotiate the asking price down, citing the lack of documentation. The final outcome of the transaction is not recorded in the evidence available to K4-Technology, so no final BUY / DO NOT BUY result is claimed here.

The practical lesson: the borescope did its job — it showed the visible liners were not obviously damaged. It could not, on its own, answer what the buyer most needed to know: how old the engine really was and how much maintenance exposure came with it.

How should a borescope inspection of a Jenbacher be organised?

  1. Agree the scope before the visit. All cylinders, not a sample. On a J320 that means 20 cylinders.
  2. Label every cylinder. Mark the cylinder number on the engine and state it at the start of each recording, so every frame can be traced to a cylinder.
  3. Record video, not only photos. Video in motion lets a reviewer distinguish a scratch from a light reflection. Still frames often cannot.
  4. Keep original files. Messenger apps compress images and can hide fine surface defects. Transfer original-resolution files.
  5. Capture context. Photograph the nameplates and the controller hour display during the same visit, so the internal images are tied to the identified machine.
  6. Write the findings per cylinder. A table: cylinder number, liner, piston crown, valve area, deposits, remarks. Images without a written assessment are hard to use later — for the buyer, the lender or in a dispute.
  7. Review the full set. A reviewer who has seen five frames out of two hundred has not reviewed the engine.

What should be combined with a borescope inspection?

Oil analysisWear metals, coolant contamination, fuel dilution — evidence from bearings and parts the camera cannot see
Compression or leak-down test per cylinderSealing of rings and valves, measured rather than seen
Controller dataOperating hours, starts, alarms, trips, load history
Service historyWhat was replaced, when, and on how many hours
Load testHow the engine behaves under representative load: cylinder temperatures, balance, alarms

K4-Technology treats borescope as one layer of evidence. The decision-grade conclusion comes from whether all layers tell the same story.

Which borescope findings should stop or change a purchase?

  • Deep vertical scoring on one or more liners.
  • Burn marks, erosion or cracking on piston crowns.
  • Heavy or uneven deposits that differ strongly between cylinders.
  • Visible corrosion inside the cylinders — a sign of poor preservation during storage.
  • A difference between cylinders or between the two banks that has no explanation.
  • The seller will not allow all cylinders to be inspected, or will not release original video.

None of these automatically means do not buy. Each should be converted into further diagnosis, a repair budget, a price adjustment or a condition of payment.

When is a remote review enough, and when do you need an on-site inspection?

A remote review is sufficient to:

  • assess borescope photos and video already supplied by the seller;
  • check whether all cylinders are covered and labelled;
  • identify frames that need original video or re-inspection;
  • define the scope of a physical inspection.

An on-site inspection is required to:

  • perform the borescope inspection with a controlled scope and labelling;
  • tie the images to the nameplates and controller of the identified machine;
  • take oil samples and compression or leak-down readings;
  • observe the engine running under load.

Frequently asked questions

Is a borescope inspection enough to buy a used Jenbacher? No. A borescope inspection shows the visible combustion-space surfaces. It does not show bearings or the crankshaft, and it does not establish operating hours or remaining life. It should be combined with oil analysis, compression data, controller data and service history.

What does a normal cylinder liner look like on a borescope? A liner in a normal wear state usually still shows the cross-hatch honing pattern in the area inspected, without deep scoring, burn marks or spalling. Interpretation should always be made by an engine specialist from original-resolution images or video.

How many cylinders should be inspected? All of them. A Jenbacher J320 has 20 cylinders; a defect in one cylinder can be missed entirely by a sample inspection.

Can I rely on borescope photos sent by the seller? They are useful as a first screen. Before relying on them, confirm that every cylinder is covered and labelled, that the images belong to the machine being sold, and request the original video files.

Does a borescope inspection require dismantling the engine? No. K4-Technology introduces the probe through the spark-plug opening, so the cylinder head does not need to be removed. The engine must be shut down and safe to work on.
