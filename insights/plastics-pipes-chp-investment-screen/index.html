Plastics Pipes CHP: Testing the Value of Recovered Heat

The investment question

When does a high-utilisation captive-power unit justify CHP, and what evidence is needed before recovered heat can be counted in the investment case?

Reported configuration and duty

Reported: A single gas-converted, 100 kWe generator operated in parallel with the utility at a plastics pipes and products factory. A former project representative described the engine as a converted diesel-origin platform, with the plant supplying part of the site's electricity and heat for space heating and hot water.

The participant recalled typical electrical output around 80–90 kWe and operation for approximately 7,500–8,000 hours per year. Applying those ranges yields 600–720 MWh/year of arithmetic generation before auxiliary-load adjustments. This is calculated from interview ranges, not metered production.

Reported outcome: The former representative recalls that the owner achieved its target payback window. The target, cash-flow statement and actual payback period were not supplied, so no verified payback, IRR or NPV is claimed.

What the scenario does and does not show

Illustrative scenario: The accompanying screening logic uses assumed CAPEX and grid-price inputs to test simple payback. It excludes a monetised heat credit because thermal energy, seasonal demand, displaced heat cost and utilisation were not metered. It is not a reconstruction of the owner's historical return.

A CHP label does not itself create bankable heat revenue. The heat system may add value, but only if the heat is delivered when needed, displaces a real alternative and is measured on a consistent boundary. Electricity and heat savings must not be double-counted.

Investor diligence gates

  • Obtain interval load and import data to establish self-consumption and residual grid charges.
  • Confirm net output after auxiliaries, gas quality, pressure and measured fuel consumption.
  • Review interconnection permissions, synchronisation and protection settings.
  • Meter recovered heat and compare it with the actual tariff and alternative heat supply.
  • Verify CAPEX scope, maintenance schedule, overhaul costs and owner cash-flow records.
  • Normalise electricity, gas and CAPEX VAT/tax treatment before calculating returns.

For a prospective fund or developer, these records are the difference between a useful operating recollection and an underwritable CHP cash-flow case.

Evidence boundary

The project details and outcome are retrospective interview reports. No OEM documents, utility bills, plant logs, supplier invoices, contracts or financial statements were reviewed. Operating-hour generation is calculated from reported ranges; the economics are illustrative. The historical project is not represented as contracted or delivered by K4. The site and owner are not named.
