Electroplating Captive Power: Match Generation to the Process Window

A recalled grid-parallel gas-engine retrofit illustrates the value of sizing to a steady load and checking when the grid still matters.

K4 TechnologyInterview-based analysis · 2026
REPORTEDCALCULATEDILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO

A production cycle and a maintenance window can make captive generation easier to screen. The historical tariff, cost, CAPEX and achieved return are not documented.

Context and decision question

Reported: A former supplier-side participant described a gas-converted generator serving an electroplating process in parallel with the grid. The line had a predictable production and maintenance rhythm, and no additional heat was needed. The previous supplier organisation sold and serviced the installation. K4 prepared this interview-based retrospective in 2026; it had no historical delivery or O&M role and performed no audit.

Decision question: At the process load and actual running hours, does on-site electricity displace charges the owner can avoid, after service, fuel, overhaul and remaining grid costs are correctly defined?

Evidence and arithmetic

Reported: The converted unit was rated at 100 kWe; approximate process demand was 80–90 kW. The line reportedly ran for 30 days followed by two days of maintenance; generator servicing was said to align with the line stop. Annual generator running time was recalled at approximately 7,500 hours. These values are interview recollections, not plant records.

Calculated: Using the 85 kW midpoint and recalled hours gives 637.5 MWh/year of illustrative generation. A repeating 30/32-day production window yields 8,212.5 theoretical hours/year. The difference of approximately 713 hours from recalled engine hours is unexplained; it is not measured forced downtime or a validated availability metric. The choice to omit heat credit follows the reported process requirement, not a comparative CHP study.

Illustrative owner economics

Reported / reconstructed: The workbook derives a RUB 3.5 million historical owner price from a recalled RUB 3.0 million reference and RUB 0.5 million increment. There is no purchase invoice. An approximate owner generation cost of RUB 2.1–2.2/kWh is recalled to include the former supplier's margin, but its components and VAT basis are not established. The tariff range in the model comes from a comparison with another interview and an illustrative escalation; it is not this customer's verified invoice.

Illustrative scenario: At fixed calculated output of 637,500 kWh/year, assumed grid tariff RUB 5.00–5.35/kWh, assumed generation cost RUB 2.20–2.10/kWh, and full avoidance of that grid tariff, the workbook calculates RUB 1.785–2.072 million/year of gross annual savings and 1.69–1.96 years of simple payback. The midpoint is about RUB 1.928 million/year and 1.81 years. These are sensitivity results, not achieved savings or the owner's historical payback. Fuel or supplier service cost must not be deducted again if already included in the assumed unit cost. Network charges and VAT must be compared on like-for-like bases.

What the screen changes

The process load and planned shutdowns give a plausible basis for a power-only screen. Before relying on its economics, request interval load and output records, gas bills, the actual avoidable tariff and connection charges, service scope, CAPEX invoice and tax treatment. K4's Gas Engine Advisory for Power Developers applies this owner-side sequence to a current project. CTA: Discuss the load and tariff basis for your site.

Evidence limits: No primary meter, invoice, cash-flow or service records were reviewed. The commissioning period is inferred, not documented; omit it publicly. The gas-use comparison with a different installation is not a validated engine efficiency curve. No actual owner return is known.
