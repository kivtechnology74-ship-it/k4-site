Variable Industrial Loads: Test the Grid and Overhaul Exposure

A reported manufacturing installation shows why a tariff premium cannot substitute for load, network and lifecycle evidence.

K4 TechnologyInterview-based analysis · 2026
REPORTEDCALCULATEDILLUSTRATIVE SCENARIO

An interview describes stops, major overhauls and a later dispute. The failure causes, legal outcome and actual investment return are unknown.

Context and decision question

Reported: A former supplier-side participant described a containerised installation of two 100 kWe gas-converted engines for a steel-microfiber process. The customer's recalled outlay was RUB 9 million including the container. The process reportedly ran 16 hours/day, with sawtooth machining demand and typical loading of 60–70% of installed electrical capacity. Electricity bought through intermediaries was said to carry a 40–50% premium over the regional average, but the underlying bills were not provided. The previous supplier delivered and serviced the historical asset; K4 prepared this retrospective analysis in 2026 and did not inspect or audit it.

Decision question: Can the proposed generators, grid interface and owner operating team handle the real load transitions, and does the investment still work when overhaul and service exposure is included?

Reported operating account and missing proof

Reported: The participant described frequent technical stops, burnt valves and cylinder-head failures, with both engines receiving major overhaul at approximately 6,000 engine hours. The local network was described as poor; customer staff handled daily operation and the prior supplier handled monthly and emergency service. A court dispute reportedly followed the overhauls. Its claims, liability and result are unknown. There are no fault logs, power-quality measurements, failure analyses, service records or court documents in this package. These accounts do not establish a root cause, breach or legally determined fault.

Calculated: The stated daily schedule creates a 5,840-hour annual ceiling per engine only if operated every day of a non-leap year. Actual annual engine hours and generated electricity are unknown. The reported load band implies 120–140 kW combined while running; this is not a measured time series.

Scenario screen, not historical profit and loss

Illustrative scenario: The workbook's reference deliberately assumes 300 production days/year, 80% of each production window running, 65 kW per engine, a RUB 5/kWh regional tariff proxy, a 45% premium, and 90% tariff avoidability. This produces 3,840 hours per engine, 499.2 MWh/year, and a modelled avoided rate of RUB 6.525/kWh. At assumed gas, routine service and recurring overhaul reserves, the model calculates RUB 1.164 million/year net benefit and 7.73 years simple payback. The actual owner did not necessarily achieve any of these values.

The assumed RUB 600,000 per-engine overhaul is a proxy from another interview, not this site's repair invoice. Applying it every 6,000 hours is a stress assumption based on a single reported event, not proof of a repeating failure interval. Under otherwise identical reference inputs, replacing that reserve interval with a 15,000-hour comparator changes the modelled annual reserve from RUB 0.768 million to RUB 0.307 million. The comparator is historical experience elsewhere, not an OEM warranty. Actual annual output, net cash flow and payback remain unknown. The model also omits financing, taxes, cash timing and some fixed costs; different VAT bases could invalidate the rate comparison.

What the owner should verify

Obtain high-resolution load and start-up traces; power-quality and protection evidence; dispatch and trip records; operator procedures; service responsibilities, quotations and overhaul invoices; and the exact components of the tariff that can be avoided. The paired laundry account offers a descriptive comparison, without assigning causation. K4's Gas Engine Advisory for Power Developers sets out the existing owner-side project scope. CTA: Send the load curve and grid constraints for an owner-side screen.

Evidence limits: Interview claims have not been independently verified. A dispute does not establish who was responsible. The account does not prove a financial loss or that the investment failed to recover its capital.
