Dependable IT MW for AI Data Centers

Model, inputs and comparison tables behind the time-to-dependable-power framework.

Igor Shibanov · Founder and Technical AdvisorVersion 1.0 · 27 October 2026US screening basis · project studies required
EQUATIONS SHOWNSOURCE TAGSLIMITATIONS STATED

Purpose. This note documents the numbers behind Time to Dependable Power: how installed generation converts to dependable IT megawatts, how redundancy translates into deficit hours and events, how delay is valued, and how the main power options compare. All modelled results are screening estimates for US conditions in 2026; project decisions require site-specific studies.

Key definition

Dependable IT MW is the IT load the full fuel-to-IT system can serve while meeting a stated obligation, expressed as annual unserved IT minutes, maximum events per year and longest permitted event.

1. From installed generation to dependable IT MW

One dependable IT MW requires 1.4–2.1 MW of installed generation for the islanded gas-engine screening case below [K4 MODEL].

Factor Low High Note
Peak PUE, liquid-cooled AI 1.15 1.35 Size on peak, not annual average
Station auxiliaries, UPS and transformer losses 1.04 1.08 Add gas compression where required
Site derate, ambient and altitude 1.03 1.18 Typically larger for turbines than engines; use project data
Reserve: N+2 on 10–12 required units 1.17 1.20 14/12 = 1.167; 12/10 = 1.20. Separate maintenance reserve raises the factor
Product 1.44 2.06 Rounded to 1.4–2.1 for screening

At $1,500–2,500 per installed kW for a reciprocating-engine plant, generation capital is $2.2–5.2M per dependable IT MW. At 12% over 15 years:

CRF = r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1) = 0.147   (r = 0.12, n = 15)

Annual capital charge is $0.32–0.76M per dependable IT MW-year. Fuel, fixed O&M and per-hour O&M are excluded. If only part of IT capacity is contracted, divide by the contracted share.

Scope. The 1.4–2.1 conversion applies only to this islanded gas-engine case. Grid, fuel cells, CCGT and BESS hybrids each need their own PUE, derate, reserve and storage-duration model.

2. N+k availability and event model

The binomial model gives expected capacity-deficit hours; event counts and durations require repair-time assumptions [K4 MODEL]. Required units k = 10, installed n = 11, 12 or 13, and each unit has forced unavailability q.

Pdef = Σ C(n,j) qj(1−q)n−j, for j = n−k+1 … n;   Hdef = 8,760 × Pdef

For the two-state Markov approximation, μ = 1/MTTR and λ = μq/(1−q), so MTBF = MTTR(1−q)/q. At the deficit boundary d = n−k+1:

fdef ≈ 8,760 × C(n,d) qd(1−q)n−d × dμ;   mean duration = Hdef/fdef

Raising MTTR from 24 to 72 hours at constant q also raises MTBF from 1,176 to 3,528 hours. This is not the same engine repaired more slowly; it compares two fleets with different failure frequency and repair duration but identical 98% availability.

From plant events to IT events

This is a concept model, not a fitted result. Unit trips are split into trips while sufficient capacity remains (Ntrip,ok) and trips initiating a deficit state (fdef), so nothing is counted twice:

EIT = Ntrip,okprt + fdef[prt + (1−prt)(1−pcurt)]

prt is the probability that a trip is not ridden through; pcurt is the probability that a deficit is met by controlled curtailment. Neither is published by operators [DATA GAP]. Both belong in the EPC or PPA as plant-boundary guarantees.

Exclusions: planned maintenance, common-mode events such as gas supply or shared controls, and unit start failures. All make real results worse than the independent-failure table.

3. Delay value and outage exposure

Delay value ranges from approximately $0.14M to $1.7M per dependable IT MW-month depending on the party. The net 15-year PV of moving the modelled plant from N+1 to N+2 for curtailable training is −$0.33M to +$0.54M per dependable IT MW [K4 MODEL].

The range spans zero, so the result is borderline rather than a case for or against redundancy. Avoided exposure alone is $0.1–0.7M PV and must not be quoted as the net benefit. Inference and SLA-bound loads are outside this curtailment model; contractual consequences dominate there.

Bridge-power test

Δt × Vdelay > Cbridge + Obridge − Rresidual + ΔEoutage

Δt is months gained; Vdelay is selected for the party paying; C and O include mobilization, permits and removal; R is the value of bridge assets retained as permanent reserve; ΔE is extra outage exposure during the bridge.

Context only: Uptime reports power caused 45% of operators’ most impactful outages in 2025, with 20% costing more than $1M [INDUSTRY REPORTING]. Meta’s 466 Llama 3 job interruptions in 54 days were mostly component failures, not power events; they show checkpoint recovery, not that facility-wide loss of power is cheap [OPERATOR DISCLOSURE].

5. Limitations and open data

  • Confirmed: Williams reports Socrates at 556 MW ISO generation capacity excluding BESS; the boundary against 400 MW of stated project capacity remains unreconciled publicly.
  • Confirmed: EIA Table 11.1 reports 269.2 SAIDI minutes, approximately 4.49 hours per customer, for 2025 with major event days. Projects should use the serving utility’s own SAIDI/SAIFI and interconnection studies.
  • Open: public wholesale rent level for the powered-shell scenario.
  • Open: independent step-load tests for loaded gas engines under cyclic AI load.

Known model limitations: independent failures; no planned-maintenance overlap; no common-mode events; installed-cost estimates extend beyond the last primary public capital-cost dataset; evidence is US-centered; achieved availability of operating AI behind-the-meter plants is not public.
