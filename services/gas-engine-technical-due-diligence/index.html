K4 provides evidence-based technical due diligence for buyers of used and rebuilt gas engines, gensets and CHP equipment.

We review the exact machine being offered — not just the model family.

The objective is to establish:

  • what the asset actually is;
  • whether the documentation matches the physical machine;
  • what its real lifecycle history appears to be;
  • what technical risks remain unresolved;
  • what maintenance exposure the buyer may inherit;
  • and whether those risks should change the price, payment terms or the decision to buy.

Jenbacher J320 / J420 / J612 / J620 and other gas-engine platforms.

Who This Service Is For

This service is designed for:

  • buyers of used gas engines and complete gensets;
  • developers building distributed-generation projects;
  • industrial companies acquiring CHP assets;
  • investors evaluating operating energy assets;
  • EPC contractors sourcing second-hand equipment;
  • buyers dealing with equipment traders, dismantled plants or rebuilt engines.

A machine does not need to be visibly damaged to represent a poor transaction.

The biggest risks are often incomplete lifecycle history, undocumented repairs, configuration mismatch, hidden maintenance exposure or an asset that cannot be verified before payment.

What K4 Verifies

Asset Identity

We reconcile:

  • engine nameplate;
  • genset nameplate;
  • generator nameplate;
  • serial numbers;
  • exact engine version;
  • electrical output;
  • voltage and frequency;
  • gas configuration;
  • controller generation;
  • supplied balance of plant.

A specification for “a J320” is not evidence that the machine offered is the same J320 described in the documents.

Lifecycle History

We reconstruct, where evidence allows:

  • original commissioning;
  • total known operating history;
  • current engine hours;
  • engine / short-block / long-block replacement;
  • major overhaul history;
  • cylinder-head and piston/liner work;
  • turbocharger history;
  • generator repairs;
  • controller replacement or hour resets;
  • shutdown and storage periods.

Displayed operating hours are treated as one data point — not automatically as total asset lifetime.

Technical Condition

Depending on scope and available evidence, review may cover:

  • visible leaks and corrosion;
  • cooling and lubrication systems;
  • ignition system;
  • exhaust manifolds;
  • turbochargers;
  • generator condition;
  • controller and alarm history;
  • borescope evidence;
  • operating data;
  • storage and preservation;
  • completeness and condition of the balance of plant.

FAT / Load-Test Review

A short start-up video is not a meaningful acceptance test.

Where a FAT or load test is available, K4 reviews the test structure and evidence against the agreed transaction requirements.

Typical review areas include:

  • stable operation;
  • staged loading;
  • agreed high-load operation;
  • electrical output;
  • temperatures and pressures;
  • cylinder and exhaust-temperature balance;
  • alarms and trips;
  • vibration and abnormal noise;
  • leaks;
  • generator behaviour;
  • auxiliary-system performance.

Acceptance criteria should be defined before the test, not after a problem appears.

Fuel and Configuration Compatibility

We review whether the exact offered configuration is compatible with the intended project based on available technical evidence.

This may include:

  • previous fuel;
  • intended fuel;
  • gas quality;
  • gas pressure;
  • required gas treatment;
  • electrical configuration;
  • potential conversion work;
  • potential derating;
  • emissions configuration.

Generic model-family marketing material is not sufficient evidence of compatibility for a specific engine.

K4 Transaction Decision Framework

Every review should lead to a commercial decision — not simply “good engine” or “bad engine”.

BUY

Identity, lifecycle, condition and performance evidence are sufficiently coherent. Remaining technical exposure is understood and reflected in the transaction.

BUY WITH CONDITIONS

The asset remains attractive, but specific evidence, repair work, testing or documentation must be provided before final payment.

PRICE ADJUSTMENT

Identified risks or upcoming technical costs are measurable enough to support a commercial adjustment.

DO NOT BUY

The asset cannot be verified adequately, the seller blocks reasonable technical checks, lifecycle history cannot be reconciled, or the risk-adjusted economics no longer make sense.
